    II The Cities

    Order · Craft · Water · Cultivation · Civic lifeA civilisation remembered for what it built.

    Streets laid out by plan. Drains beneath them. Wells in the courtyards. Water, everywhere, given order.

    And at the heart of the city, a place made only for bathing.

    There was a time when care was not separated from life. Water prepared the body. Oil protected it.

    The plan

    Indus cities such as Mohenjo-daro were built on grids of baked brick, with covered drainage running beneath the streets.

    The Great Bath

    A watertight brick pool with steps at either end — among the earliest public water structures known. Its exact use is still debated.

    III The Crossing

    Ships left one shore carrying stone, timber, metal, fibre — the work of human hands.

    They reached another, carrying proof that distance could be crossed.

    On clay tablets in Mesopotamia, the far land they came from was given a name.

    Meluhha.

    The ships

    Sea trade linked the Indus region with the Gulf and Mesopotamia — through ports remembered as Magan and Dilmun.

    The name

    Many scholars associate “Meluhha” with the Indus civilisation and the regions linked to it. Its exact boundaries remain a matter of scholarship.

    VII The Ritual

    Care, given a rhythm.

    1. 01Prepare

      Bathe, then pat the skin until it remains slightly damp.

    2. 02Warm

      Pour a small measure into the palm and let body heat loosen the oil.

    3. 03Press

      Place it where the skin feels dry before beginning to spread.

    4. 04Draw

      Use long, unhurried strokes until the skin feels supple, not wet.

    5. 05Pause

      Let the oil settle before dressing or layering fragrance.

    Ritual is care made visible through repetition.

    Why the ritual works

    Oil does not add water to the skin. Used after bathing, an emollient body oil helps soften the surface and reduce the escape of existing moisture. That is why timing matters as much as quantity.
